Release v3.2 — August 12, 2026

Everything shipped on igovazd.am since v2.9 (August 7), in one place. It covers what
v3.0 and v3.1 described as well as everything that went out after them, so this file
can be read on its own.

— Your account —
1. One account can now be both sides of the marketplace. Selling your own project and
   buying placement in someone else's used to need two separate sign-ups with two
   different email addresses. There is now a switch under your avatar: turn on the other
   side and it is available immediately, with the same login. What you can do follows
   what you switched on, not what you picked the day you registered.
2. You cannot apply to your own listing, or save it to favourites. Before, a creator who
   also had a brand account could send themselves an application. The button and the
   heart are now simply absent on anything you own.
3. Signing in as a member no longer signs you out of the staff panel, and the other way
   round. When a browser holds both, each menu carries a link across to the other one, so
   neither session is stranded behind the other.
4. Nobody is invited to register an account they are already using. The two buttons on
   the creators page and the matching pair on the home page pointed at the sign-up form
   whoever clicked them. They now lead where the click actually means: the sign-up form
   for a visitor, the submit form for a member who already sells, their own cabinet for
   one who only buys. The sign-in and sign-up pages themselves also stop a signed-in
   member from landing on a form they filled in long ago.
5. Signing in from a project page brings you back to that project. Following the sign-in
   link from a listing and then logging in used to drop you in your cabinet, losing the
   page you were reading. It now returns you to where you started — including the case
   where you came to apply for one particular offer.
6. The cabinet has the site's real footer. It used to be a wordmark, a copyright line and
   support contacts, so Terms, Privacy, About and the FAQ were unreachable from inside
   your account. The only column left out is the one whose links you already have in your
   own navigation.

— Advertising channels and ad space —
7. A placement now says what kind of integration it is. A brand could see that a project
   sells "product placement" but not whether the deal was a product on screen, a logo in
   the credits, a verbal mention in dialogue, or naming rights over the whole series. A
   placement carries one of those four kinds, with the meaning spelled out next to the
   picker in the form. The catalog card shows a project's kinds as their own row of
   chips, and the catalog gained a filter for them. Placements written before this have
   no kind, and that is a normal, sellable offer: nothing was guessed or backfilled.
8. A new section, `/ads`, opens all nine advertising channels the platform sells, not
   just film placement. Each channel has its own page — what it is, what can be bought,
   and a showcase. Placement and Events show the projects that already exist; the other
   seven channels don't have an inventory yet and say so plainly, with a link to get in
   touch, instead of showing invented listings.
9. Advertising space outside a film is now for sale. Billboards, lift posters, transit,
   radio, TV, video and banner inventory get their own listings, with photos, location,
   size or format, daily reach and availability dates. A creator adds them in "My ad
   spaces"; they go through the same review queue as a project and appear on their
   channel's page once approved.
10. City is picked from a list instead of typed. Typing it by hand meant the same city
    arrived spelled three ways and stopped matching the filter. The list covers 20 cities
    and still lets you enter one that isn't there. The creator's own list of spaces also
    shows the city in the reader's language, which was the one place it stayed English.
11. An ad space is now looked after like a project. Every save writes a version you can
    roll back to, a deleted space goes to the same bin and can be restored with its
    offers, submitting one writes to the moderators and a moderation answer writes to its
    owner (until now a rejected space produced a red dot and no letter), deleting one
    clears its files from disk unless another live listing shows the same picture, and
    the list can be dragged into the order you want.
12. Messages on the ad-space form say what they mean. Submitting a billboard and being
    told "the project cannot be submitted for review" was confusing, and one message was
    outright wrong: a permission refusal showed "fill in the title, synopsis and at least
    one genre".

— The catalog —
13. The catalog shows every kind of advertising in one place — projects and ad space
    together — and the filters are built from what is actually listed, so you never pick
    a filter that returns nothing.
14. The project card says what each value is. Format used to lead with the genres and
    look identical to them, so "Sci-Fi · Drama · +2 · Mini-series" read as four genres
    when only the last one is what the project is; format now comes first with its own
    chip. The platform label shares a line with its values instead of sitting on a row of
    its own. A project open to applications with no closing date said a bare "Ongoing";
    it now names itself like every other line. A release date entered to the day was
    being shown as month and year; the day now survives, and dates saved less precisely
    are untouched. Runtime reads as clock time rather than a spec sheet's "100 min".
15. Result counts are grammatical in Russian and Armenian. "2 рекламных мест" is now
    "2 рекламных места". When nothing matches, the whole count line is hidden instead of
    leaving a dangling "Showing" above the empty-state block.
16. The platform filter printed one entry in Armenian to Russian and English readers.
    Fixed in the listings themselves, so it reads correctly in all three languages.
17. Countries are shown in your language everywhere, and one project's country list
    carried an entry that had no meaning outside Armenian; it has been removed.
18. An empty catalog explains itself. Filters survive a reload for the rest of the
    browser session, so someone coming back later could meet an empty result with no
    visible cause — on a phone the ticked boxes sit inside a closed sheet. "Clear all"
    now sits in the empty state itself, next to the dead end.

— The project page —
19. Standard package and placement names read in your language. On all nine live projects
    the three default positions carried Armenian names only, so a Russian or English
    reader saw Armenian package names inside an otherwise translated page.
20. The back control at the top of a project no longer reads "← Back Catalog". The arrow
    and the word said the same thing twice before naming the destination; it is now the
    arrow plus where it goes, with the wording kept for screen readers.
21. The Print button is gone from phones. Printing a project page from a phone opens a
    system dialog for something nobody prints from a phone, and the button was taking a
    third of the first row a brand sees. Share stays.
22. The production timeline stands up on a phone. Ten stages laid out sideways made a
    rail six and a half screens wide: reading a list of dates meant dragging across it.
    Below phone width the stages now run top to bottom, one per row. The desktop layout
    is unchanged.
23. Offer cards are a size smaller on phones. One card used to fill the whole screen, and
    a project sells up to nine of them; two now fit where one did.

— Getting around —
24. A brand no longer loses the whole menu when leaving the buyer cabinet. Opening the
    catalog, a project page or the advertising section left the top bar empty; the two
    cabinet links now follow you everywhere except the creator side.
25. The advertising section is reachable from a dropdown in the header, all nine channels
    at once, and the guide for creators is linked from the footer of every page.

— Applications from brands —
26. The confirmation screen says who answers. It read "Offer sent" and nothing else, so a
    brand waited for the project's creator — who has not been part of this chain since
    August 7. It now names the iGovazd team as the one who answers and says where the
    answer arrives: in your cabinet and by email.
27. An accepted application is called accepted. In the brand's cabinet the status read
    "Mutual interest", a name from the time when it meant the creator had expressed
    interest back.
28. A new application now emails the staff who answer it. Until now it produced an inbox
    row and a browser notification and nothing else, so a lead arriving overnight waited
    on somebody happening to open the panel. The letter names the project, the brand and
    the offer, and links to the queue; the brand's contact details stay in the panel
    rather than in the mail.
29. The creator's old application inbox is gone for good. It had been an empty redirect
    since August 7, kept alive only for notifications written before that day. Those were
    rewritten first, so no old bell entry turns into a dead link.

— Notifications —
30. If your browser held both a staff session and a member session, the member cabinet
    showed the staff inbox — someone else's notifications, with "mark all as read" acting
    on them, and admin notification text surfacing on a member's page. Each cabinet now
    reads only its own. Push subscriptions were registering under the wrong account for
    the same reason and are fixed with it.

— Submitting a project —
31. The form no longer offers differently-named ways out of the same page. The link at the
    top says where it takes you — back to your cabinet — and the buttons say what happens
    to your work: on a new project they save the draft before leaving, and they now really
    do save it rather than relying on the autosave timer. A third exit at the foot of the
    form still said "Cancel" and still dropped the draft; it now matches the others.

— The About page —
32. The founder cards shipped with invented people and invented biographies. Real names
    aren't ready, and placeholder people on a live site read worse than no section at all,
    so the cards are gone and the copy that shared the row with them stands on its own.
    The page's heading also ran far larger than every other section heading on the site
    and now matches them, and the closing block carries one button instead of two.

— Accessibility —
33. The log-out confirmation keeps the keyboard inside it. Tab used to walk straight out
    of the dialog into the page behind, which is exactly what a confirmation must not
    allow. Focus now enters the dialog when it opens, stays in it, and returns to the
    button that opened it.
34. The disabled favourites heart on your own project no longer announces "add to
    favourites" to a screen reader.

— Staff tools —
35. The dictionary editor's section list is readable. Over half the platform's interface
    text — 534 of 1002 entries — had no named section and fell back to raw key prefixes,
    entries like `forCreators.field.castPhoto.` sitting in a list of otherwise plain
    names. Every entry now lands in one of 83 human-readable sections, and a new check
    stops an unnamed section from shipping again.
36. The Members tab of the admin panel spoke Russian inside an otherwise English panel,
    because it read the visitor's language instead of the panel's.
37. The header inside a cabinet shows the cabinet's owner, not whichever session happened
    to win when a staff account and a member account shared a browser.

— Under the hood —
38. The code linter reports nothing outstanding for the first time, and its two React
    rules now block a new violation instead of adding it to a backlog of forty-odd. Five
    of the findings were real defects rather than style: the editors for cast, milestones
    and offers were reading a counter at the wrong moment, and three background tasks
    were declaring the wrong inputs.
39. The end-to-end test suite tests this site again. It assumed a test account existed
    with a known password on whatever machine ran it, and it was pointed at a port that
    another application answers on — so it had been quietly checking somebody else's app
    and failing four of its own checks on a refused login. Both of the defects fixed in
    items 5 and 31 were found by putting it right.
40. Page scrolling stopped three separate pieces of code competing for the same frame.
41. Interface wording was corrected in four rounds by the platform's own translator,
    straight from the editor in the admin panel — 19 entries across Armenian, Russian and
    English.
42. A security update to one of the platform's image-handling components.
